Project

Heart Finite Element Model

Finite element simulation of aortic valve stenosis: modeling blood flow, pressure drops, and mesh convergence for treatment insights.

Project Snapshot

  • Tech Stack: Finite Element Methods (FEM), Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), Navier-Stokes Equations, Newton-Raphson Iteration, Mesh Convergence Analysis
  • Key Outcome: Engineered a mathematically convergent computational model of aortic valve stenosis capable of accurately predicting critical hemodynamic pressure gradients (~40 mmHg) to aid in personalized surgical treatment planning.

Model Setup & Mesh

Developed a computational model of a stenosed (narrowed) aortic valve using finite element methods (FEM) to simulate blood flow dynamics. Discretized the valve geometry with triangular elements and applied Gauss quadrature integration. Boundary conditions: inlet blood velocity 200 mm/s, stress-free outlet, no-slip walls at valve tissue.

Flow Physics & Velocity Profile

Simulated incompressible Navier-Stokes flow (momentum and continuity equations). Results show high-velocity jet formation through the stenosis opening, with peak velocity exceeding inlet by 5–10×. Observed recirculation zones downstream in the aortic sinuses with lower velocity regions, characteristic of stenotic flow patterns clinically observed in patients.

Pressure Drop Analysis

  • Transvalvular pressure gradient: Quantified pressure drop across stenosis as a key clinical indicator of stenosis severity.
  • Spatial pressure distribution: Identified high-pressure zones upstream and low-pressure zones in the jet core.
  • Clinical relevance: Pressure drop values aligned with known hemodynamic thresholds for symptomatic stenosis (threshold ~40 mmHg).

Mesh Refinement & Convergence

Conducted systematic mesh refinement from coarse (500 elements) to fine (5000+ elements) to ensure solution accuracy. Solved nonlinear weak formulation using Newton-Raphson iteration with each mesh resolution. Computed RMSE between successive mesh refinement levels to assess convergence.

Results & Clinical Application

  • Convergence behavior: RMSE decreased asymptotically; optimal mesh identified where computational cost no longer justified accuracy gains.
  • Validated results: Velocity and pressure fields consistent with experimental stenosis flow data from literature.
  • Clinical value: Provides surgeons personalized hemodynamic predictions for treatment planning (balloon valvuloplasty patient feasibility).
  • Mesh sensitivity: Demonstrates critical importance of mesh fidelity near stenosis opening for accurate pressure gradient prediction.
