Project

Image-Guided Navigation Simulation (IGNR)

Engineered an end-to-end surgical robotics simulation pipeline for safe, automated electrode insertion into the hippocampus.

Project Snapshot

  • Tech Stack: Python, ROS2 (Ubuntu), 3D Slicer, OpenIGTLink, MoveIt2, URDF, NumPy, VTK, SimpleITK
  • Key Outcome: Architected a complete IGNR pipeline bridging medical image processing with real-time robotic kinematics, strictly adhering to complex anatomical safety parameters.

The Objective: Precision Neurosurgery

Goal: Automate the path planning and robotic insertion of an electrode into the hippocampus to monitor temporal lobe epilepsy. This required building an end-to-end system that could interpret MRI brain segmentations, calculate a safe physical trajectory, and command a 6-DOF robotic arm to execute the movement in a simulated environment.

Algorithmic Path Planning & Safety Constraints

To ensure patient safety, the path planning algorithm (written in Python via 3D Slicer) had to calculate vectors that strictly adhered to specific medical requirements rather than just finding the shortest distance.

  • Hard Constraints: The trajectory must successfully target the hippocampus, completely avoid ventricles and blood vessels, and maintain an insertion angle greater than 55° from the cortex to prevent needle deflection.
  • Soft Constraints & Optimization: To maximize the safety margin, I utilized Danielsson distance maps and subdivided the spatial data into Octrees for highly efficient collision detection, selecting the trajectory that maximized the distance from all critical structures.

System Architecture & Real-Time Integration

Connecting static medical imaging to dynamic robotics required a robust communication bridge.

  • Data Streaming: Implemented the OpenIGTLink protocol to establish a server/client connection, streaming the calculated trajectory as a linear transform from 3D Slicer directly into the ROS2 environment.
  • Robotic Kinematics: Designed a custom 6-DOF virtual robot using URDF. Configured the joint limits, collision geometry, and kinematics within MoveIt2 and RViz to receive the spatial data and execute the planned insertion path.

Validation & Engineering Insights

The entire pipeline was validated through a rigorous testing framework, including unit tests for coordinate transformations (RAS to IJK) and integration tests for the collision-avoidance algorithms. The results highlighted two key engineering insights for future clinical iterations:

  • Biological Weighting: The initial soft constraint weighted vessel and ventricle avoidance equally. However, damaging a blood vessel carries a much higher risk of hemorrhage compared to ventricle damage. Future optimization functions must apply an anatomically informed weighting ratio (e.g., 60/40).
  • Mesh vs. Point Clouds: Generating continuous meshes from discrete voxel data introduced smoothing inaccuracies at the tissue boundaries. Transitioning the visualization from meshes to raw point clouds will provide higher-resolution collision detection at the microscopic level.
